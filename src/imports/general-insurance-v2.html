General Insurance in India

General insurance covers what you own and specific risks you face — your vehicle, your home, or a trip — rather than your life or your medical bills. Policies run for a fixed short term, usually a year, and the payout is tied to the value of what is damaged, lost or disrupted. In India, only third-party motor cover is legally mandatory.

The basics

What does general insurance actually cover?

General insurance is an umbrella term for every policy that protects an asset or an event rather than a person's life or health. In practice, for most households in India it comes down to three products: motor, home and travel.

The structure differs from life insurance in two important ways. First, the term is short. A life policy may run for thirty years; a general insurance policy usually runs for one, and lapses if you do not renew it. Second, the payout is indemnity-based. You are not paid a fixed sum on a defined event — you are compensated for the actual loss, up to the sum insured. A car worth ₹6 lakh does not produce a ₹10 lakh claim, however high the premium.

That makes general insurance a different kind of decision from term insurance. Life cover is about protecting people who depend on your income. General insurance is about protecting the things that would be expensive to replace, and the events that would be expensive to absorb.

Motor

Covers your vehicle and your liability to others. Third-party cover is required by law; own-damage cover is what protects your own car. Premium depends largely on the vehicle's insured declared value.

Home

Covers the building, the contents, or both. IRDAI's standard home product, Bharat Griha Raksha, gives a common baseline across insurers, which makes comparing quotes considerably easier.

Travel

Covers medical emergencies abroad, trip cancellation, baggage loss and delays. Medical cover is the part that matters most — treatment costs overseas are the single largest risk on most trips.

The legal position

Which general insurance is mandatory in India?

Only one: third-party motor insurance. Under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, every vehicle used in a public place must carry third-party liability cover. Driving without it is a punishable offence, and the absence of cover does not remove your liability — it simply means you pay the claim yourself.

Everything else is optional as a matter of law, but often mandatory as a matter of contract. Home loan lenders routinely require property insurance for the duration of the loan. Schengen visa applications require travel medical cover of at least €30,000. Neither is a legal requirement on you as a citizen; both are conditions imposed by whoever you are dealing with.

Set the record straight

What do people get wrong about general insurance?

Common myth

Is third-party motor insurance enough?

No. Third-party cover pays for damage and injury you cause to other people and their property. Your own vehicle is not covered at all.

If your car is hit in an accident, stolen, submerged in a flood or damaged by fire, a third-party policy pays nothing towards repairing or replacing it. That protection comes from own-damage cover, which is what turns a third-party policy into a comprehensive one. For a vehicle worth several lakh rupees, the gap between the two premiums is usually far smaller than the loss it protects against.

Common myth

Can renters buy home insurance?

Yes. Home insurance treats the structure and the contents as two separate things, and a tenant can insure the second without owning the first.

Furniture, appliances, electronics and personal belongings are just as exposed to fire, theft and water damage in a rented flat as in an owned one — and in a rented flat, the landlord's policy almost never covers them. Contents-only cover is inexpensive precisely because it excludes the building.

Common myth

Is credit card travel insurance enough?

Rarely, and the reason is the sum insured rather than the cover itself.

Card-linked travel cover is typically capped well below what a hospital admission in the United States or Europe can cost. It also commonly excludes pre-existing conditions and adventure activities, and may only apply if the trip was booked on that card. The cover is not worthless, but treat it as a supplement and read the sum insured before you rely on it.

Making the decision

How do you choose the right cover?

1

Start with what you could not afford to replace

Insurance is worth buying where the loss would be materially painful, not where it would be merely annoying. A vehicle, a home and an overseas medical bill qualify. A phone usually does not.

2

Check the sum insured, not the premium

Two policies at similar premiums can differ enormously in what they will actually pay. The sum insured, and how it is calculated, determines the size of your claim.

3

Read the exclusions before the features

Add-ons are marketed; exclusions are disclosed. The exclusions list tells you what a policy will refuse to pay, and it is where most claim disputes originate.

4

Look at the insurer's claim settlement record

IRDAI publishes claim settlement data by insurer in its annual report. A cheaper premium from an insurer with a weak settlement record is not a saving.

5

Fit it into the rest of your plan

General insurance protects assets. It sits alongside life cover for your dependants and estate planning for what happens to those assets afterwards. Gaps usually appear at the joins.

Questions

General insurance FAQs

Is general insurance mandatory in India?

Only third-party motor insurance is legally mandatory, under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988. Driving without it is an offence. Home and travel insurance are optional, though home loan lenders usually require property cover and several visa regimes require a minimum level of travel medical cover.

How long does a general insurance policy last?

Most general insurance policies run for a fixed short term and are then renewed. Motor policies typically run one year for own-damage cover. Home policies commonly run one to five years. Travel policies run for the length of a single trip, or a year for a multi-trip plan.

Can I claim on two policies for the same loss?

You can hold two policies covering the same asset, but general insurance is indemnity-based, so you cannot profit from a loss. Where two policies cover the same event, the insurers share the claim in proportion to their sums insured. You recover the loss once, not twice.

What is not covered under general insurance?

Common exclusions include normal wear and tear, deliberate damage, and consequential losses. Motor policies exclude driving without a valid licence or under the influence of alcohol. Travel policies frequently exclude pre-existing medical conditions unless they have been declared and accepted. The policy wording is the authority in each case.

Does home insurance cover a rented property?

It depends on who is buying it. A landlord insures the structure. A tenant insures their own contents. Neither policy covers the other's interest automatically, which is why a tenant relying on the landlord's cover is usually uninsured for their own belongings.

How much travel insurance cover do I need?

Start with the destination's requirement, then check whether it is realistic. Schengen visas require at least €30,000 of medical cover. For travel to the United States, where treatment costs are substantially higher, a materially larger sum insured is usually appropriate.
